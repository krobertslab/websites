Call for Papers

language processing across the biomedical, clinical, and health domains

BioNLP 2027 is the 26th BioNLP workshop and the 21st annual meeting of SIGBIOMED, the ACL Special Interest Group on Biomedical Natural Language Processing. We invite original research on language processing for the biomedical literature, clinical text, consumer health information, and other health-related language.

We especially welcome work on evaluation, reliability, and transparency of generative models in health settings, and on languages and populations that are under-represented in current research.

Topics of interest

  • Entity identification and normalization (linking) for a broad range of semantic categories
  • Traditional NLP tasks (e.g., NER, relation extraction, question answering, summarization, text simplification, discourse analysis, coreference resolution) for biomedical data
  • Extraction of complex relations and events
  • Text mining & Literature based discovery
  • Question answering; summarization; text simplification
  • Resources & strategies for evaluation
  • Infrastructures and pre-trained and large language models for biomedical NLP
  • Processing and annotation platforms
  • Synthetic data generation & data augmentation
  • Translating biomedical NLP research into practice
  • Reproducibility of the published findings
  • Agentic approaches for biomedical NLP
  • Reasoning, planning, and tool use in biomedical foundation models
  • Human-AI collaboration & clinician-in-the-loop
  • Evaluation & auditing of biomedical LLMs
  • Biomedical hypothesis generation, scientific discovery and automated evidence synthesis

ArchEHR-QA

The first two iterations of the ArchEHR-QA ("Archer") shared task focused on answering patients' health-related questions using their own electronic health records, with answers supported by and explicitly linked to the underlying clinical data. The task assumed the answers were generated as drafts to be reviewed and simplified for the patients by clinicians. The 2027 third iteration of the task focuses on generating patient-facing answers in lay language while preserving clinical accuracy and explicit links to supporting evidence in the supplied clinical data. The central challenge is to improve comprehensibility without distorting clinical meaning, omitting essential information, or introducing unsupported claims. This iteration will thus extend ArchEHR-QA from evidence-grounded clinical synthesis to comprehensible, evidence-grounded patient communication.
