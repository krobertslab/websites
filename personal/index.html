About

natural language processing (NLP) and information retrieval (IR) for all types of biomedical language data

I am an Associate Professor at the McWilliams School of Biomedical Informatics at UTHealth Houston, where I have been on the faculty since 2016. My research covers natural language processing (NLP), information retrieval (IR), and biomedical informatics, with a focus on making the full range of biomedical information usable by clinicians, patients, and researchers.

Before joining UTHealth Houston, I was a postdoctoral fellow at the Lister Hill National Center for Biomedical Communications at the U.S. National Library of Medicine. I have helped organize several community evaluations, including 12+ TREC tracks. I am also an organizer of the BioNLP and ClinicalNLP workshops co-located at *ACL venues.

Research

Research areas

Clinical information extraction

Electronic health records (EHRs) contain a wealth of information about a patient's medical history, but much of the richest information is contained in textual narratives. Clinical information extraction is the task of structuring this information for downstream analysis. My focus here is developing (1) methods that improve NLP performance, (2) systems that generalize across many potential use cases, and (3) approaches that reduce the time/resources needed for NLP practitioners to create real-world systems.

Question answering for EHRs

Interacting with EHRs is often burdensome. Letting clinicians and researchers ask questions of EHRs in plain language and get answers they can verify is a more natural (and hopefully effective) means of interacting with EHRs. My focus here is (1) developing trustworthy question answering (QA) methods that are verifiable and "know what they don't know", as well as (2) analyzing how to best adapt state-of-the-art large language models (LLMs) to EHR QA in a way that reflects real clinical use cases.

Biomedical information retrieval

Biomedicine is driven by data in order to generate evidence. This means there are many large text-based datasets (e.g., biomedical literature, EHR notes, clinical trials, genomic datasets). To make these FAIR (findable, accessible, interoperable, reusable), we need methods to search various types of biomedical collections. My focus here is developing shareable test collections used to evaluate biomedical search, especially through the TREC shared tasks.
